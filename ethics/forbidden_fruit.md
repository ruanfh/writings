# Genesis forbidden fruit reading

This reading makes no claim to authority.

It is limited strictly to Genesis 2:4–3:24.

## Status: Work in progress

Missing:

- Consequences of eating the fruit

## Symbolism of the senses

| Sense   | Symbolism                      |
| ------- | ------------------------------ |
| Sight   | Self-evident knowledge         |
| Hearing | Socially shared knowledge      |
| Smell   | Intuition about what is hidden |
| Taste   | Destructive analysis           |
| Touch   | Mutual exposure                |

To feel a taste one must chew, destroying the thing being tasted and evaluating its parts.

Note that one can chew to taste then spit, so ingestion is yet another symbol to be examined.

## Symbolism of the serpent

A serpent has the form of a sinuous line.
A line can join two points together while splitting two regions of the plane apart.

What also connects and separates is reason. Reasoning is a way of establishing similarities and differences.

But a sinuous line does so in a way that is not straight.
In other words, a sinuous line uses a path that can forge any separation given two points to interconnect.
Given one similarity, a sinuous line can represent the act of arbitrary selection of facts to deceive.

In fact, the serpent opens dialog with a misdirection that exaggerates the prohibition, inducing resentment:
"Did God really say that you can't eat from **any** fruit of the garden".

## Symbolism of ingestion

To ingest something is to destroy it and use its materials to enhance or degrade oneself.

## Symbolism of the fruit of good and evil

The boundary is: don't eat.
The woman mentions that the fruit shall not even be touched, but the original command was to not eat it.
Permissions that are still granted:
- See
- Talk about
- Smell

So good and evil can be known by:
- Self-evidence, patent knowledge
- Social sharing (although that is the means for the transgression)
- Intuition, latent knowledge

But it shall not be known by:
- Destructive analysis

## Symbolism of clothes

Clothes are associated with protection and social roles.
